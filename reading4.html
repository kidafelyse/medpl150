Reflection 4: Nuclear War: The Rising Risk

Much like the graphic novel: Motherhood Crisis, this article about nuclear war utilizes a different feature to immerse and hook its viewers to spread its message. Once again, it is also a stark contrast to the AI being utilized today to spread self-confirming biases rather than challenge ideas, but Nuclear War: The Rising Risk explores a unique message of its own about the danger nuclear weapons have posed in their developing tensions through the decades. It is a modernizing and expansive system, as seen in how geopolitical tensions today partly exist because of the development of more countries like North Korea, Russia, China, and Ukraine, and the tension they create with countries like the US. Through this see with our own eyes the risk, yet it's not imagined enough by people, when in reality, the escalation of nuclear war keeps growing. This article, in what I think they succeed in spreading their message in their art, is their immersion through smooth transitions and interactive medium of comic styles, integrated with a scroll-down feature to have the viewer involved. They also make use of the intensity of the color red in order to convey the severity and seriousness of the risk of nuclear war. By integrating these transitional comics in small sectors along the article, it not only creates a new experience for readers, but also a way to maintain people’s attention in the article, even when the article is already a good piece of writing. But the integration, I’ve noticed, of this immersive feature makes it so that people can still understand the severity by just color and the way they depicted nuclear war in their snippets of comics.

Not only does the comic strip help aid in getting the gist of the article just from a first glance view, but the article also encapsulates the fear one should have over the threat of nuclear war as warned by Vladimir Putin over NATO’s involvement with Ukraine. The comic strip achieves this by immersing us into a theoretical situation of a nuclear war, by putting it in a perspective that we could understand by drawing an explosion that depicts the well-known mushroom cloud, paired with a red environment only because of the red flames that burn homes and every living thing within the cloud’s radius. The overall comic conveyed a sense of constant burning, almost similar to hell, which is one way to reach an audience, as most viewers could connect with that and finally get the severity of the issue. By using common imagery like mushroom clouds and the depiction of hell, it expands the discussion from just something political to something fitting into mainstream entertainment as mainstream. This, in turn, serves the purposes of the article’s message, which is to spread awareness of the reality of the threat of nuclear war as something that one should disregard merely because of its dated relationship to the arms race between Russia and the USA during the Cold War. By utilizing such immersive imagery placements and color, one can digest the severity as more than simple words of Nuclear War, but as something we can see as well and finally connect to.
