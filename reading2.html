Reflection: With 'AI slop' Distorting our Reality, the World is Sleepwalking into Disaster by Nesrine Malik

I have had my disappointing experiences with AI, as well as concerning ones. Concerning in a sense where I would have to do a double-take in order to tell what I saw, say a mere 5-year-old beat a grandmaster at chest level on video, only to find out it was AI generated and the kid never existed. The kid was only ai-generated into a video with the chess grandmaster and it left me with a sense of loss of reality, like I no longer had control over how real I want my feelings to be. For feelings to be grounded on something meaningful, like being impressed at a kid for beating a chest grandmaster. That is inspiring in itself, it suggests that we all could achieve things at any stage of our lives. Yet with the context of the video being AI, it loses the foundation of inspiration to validate this once-amazed feeling I had. It makes me wonder if there is any media out there where I can trust my amazement over. I don’t feel safe enough to look at an animation video by someone anymore, and admire them for their hard work. Instead, I’m left wondering and suspecting them of AI generated, if not all, at least some parts. It creates a world of doubt for me which is what I would like to add to Nesrine Malik’s points. He makes a point that AI generated slop distorts our reality and I truly believe it in the symptoms of doubt it has created for me. I can no longer look at art, the same way with this doubt. It makes one wonder if this may just be a ubiquitous shift in expectations and art culture now as ubiquitous the ai slop is.

There is also a matter of political concern and not just the concern over whether or not I can appreciate art anymore but political videos distributed by supposedly-trusted administrations and governments. As mentioned in Nesrine Malik’s article, there are politicians like JD Vance and Donald Trump who don’t hesitate to post AI-Slop in order to further their political agenda, which in turn targets technologically vulnerable people into considering it to further their political biases. This is a contrast to the democratic way of things, in which we are all supposed to create an open discussion and agreement, but with the rise of self-confirming political video exacerbated by AI. Wouldn’t this only further the polarization of the country? Wouldn’t this in turn, create a divided people whose democratic foundations are crippling with the rise of falsified beliefs and redundancy of them? With it, could there be a possible new wave of art that is based on the manufactured problems that AI has made as well. While some people may still be guarded, it is inevitable there are vulnerable people out there that will believe these videos without hesitation, which in turn, can start from just one ai-video and from how ubiquitous it is, can spread for reasons counter intuitive to furthering our society’s democratic beliefs. For that to happen, people’s problems need to be grounded in something real, not in some distorted reality as Nesrine Malik calls it.
