Reflection 3: Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis

It was truly heartbreaking to see these four women’s stories as someone who already complains about the lack of universal healthcare in the US. But in putting myself in their position, as a pregnant woman living under a system built around gender discrimination, inequality, poverty, and a weak infrastructure. My problem already seems small compared to the pain and lack of local medical access that they have. From not knowing how to navigate fustula, pregnancy from rapes, lack of accessible professional medical staff, to the experiences of abuse and being pregnant in prison. It is worth noting the strength it takes to go through such situations, and in recognizing their strength, one must also take note of the medium in which people like me were able to connect and hear their stories to begin with.

This story was told through a storytelling feature that is prominent in most social media platforms, which is the stories feature, where one can display clips of their life, creating small snippets that induce immersion because of how easily one can just skim through a person’s life. It can be a misrepresentation of one’s life, and it is a feature often regarded among my generation as a way to cherry-pick their lives into curating one that people can easily digest, but also envy the poster for. But this article utilizes this cherry-picking curation feature to its advantage, as the stories of these women probably wouldn’t have been as immersive as it was if it weren’t for the riveting storytelling, paired with great art narration, and a medium of storytelling that caters to short attention spans. The tap feature that helps one skip through their lives, and gives the appearances to the viewer a sense of control over their media, but in reality, the feature as it has worked for this article, functioned rather to keep the viewer as immersed as they possibly could towards a story that would have otherwise been looked over, like the common tendency towards long-form articles. This catering towards attention spans in turn, serves the purpose of telling these stories which is to spread awareness of the struggle these women faced so others could connect and find strength through them as well.

This combination of journalism and utilizing features meant to immerse and draw viewers and readers in creates a shift in journalism as more than video form or article, but into short-form content as well, convenient for short attention spans. This way, it lets people, who probably would have never read through it, actually immerse themselves in stories that are exclusive from their experiences and bubbles. It is a contrast to the way AI generation has been utilized today. In contrast, while this form of journalism aims to create a ubiquitous arts and media to tell stories that challenge people’s views, AI utilizes its ubiquity in order to confirm people’s biases towards conservative beliefs, as it is inherently created to do. The article was an exploration towards new ways of telling stories, and it is a breath of fresh air for media compared to the AI slop that has overwhelmed our media today.
